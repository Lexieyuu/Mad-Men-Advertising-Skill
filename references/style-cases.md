# Curated style cases

These references define visual direction. Learn from their composition, color, material, and art-direction logic. Never copy their people, products, logos, wording, or exact layouts.

## Foundation: David Klein

### New York Fly TWA, 1956

Source: [Museum of Modern Art collection](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/6291)

Use for:

- bold geometric simplification
- concentrated visual energy
- strong focal hierarchy
- limited text and confident negative space
- printed travel-advertising character

Do not copy the TWA identity, Times Square subject matter, aircraft, lettering, or exact composition.

### TWA travel-poster overview

Source: [Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/david-kleins-twa-travel-posters)

Use for the common Mad Men-era foundation shared by all three modes: a single enticing scene, controlled color, commercial clarity, and visual aspiration.

## Premium commercial: Stan Galli

### New York, United Air Lines

Source: [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2007676183/)

The Library of Congress record states that this item has no known restrictions on publication. Use it to study:

- decisive commercial simplification
- one dominant visual message
- confident silhouette and product-era optimism
- economy and directness

Do not copy the airline identity, Statue of Liberty concept, or layout.

### Illustrator profile

Source: [Society of Illustrators](https://societyillustrators.org/award-winners/stanley-w-galli/)

Use Galli's directness and simplicity as a guide for Premium mode. The result should feel resolved and client-ready, not busy or exploratory.

## Editorial commercial: Mac Conner

### The Original Mad Man

Source: [Museum of the City of New York](https://www.mcny.org/exhibition/original-mad-man)

Use for:

- hand-painted postwar advertising and editorial illustration
- narrative staging inside a single scene
- expressive crops and dramatic viewpoints
- period lifestyle detail
- a clear relationship between subject, setting, and story

Do not reproduce a specific magazine story, character, advertisement, or page design.

Mac Conner is the strongest bridge between the Skill's advertising foundation and Editorial mode because his work spans advertising, popular magazines, reference photography, and preliminary design.

## Agency concept: George Lois

### George Lois Big Idea Archive

Sources: [City College of New York archive overview](https://www.ccny.cuny.edu/adpr/george-lois-big-idea-archives) and [collection description](https://www.georgeloisarchives.com/the-collection)

Use for:

- concept-first art direction
- the relationship between rough idea and finished communication
- original layouts, scripts, correspondence, photography, and design development
- concise presentation of one strong campaign idea

Do not copy an Esquire cover, logo, slogan, celebrity concept, or finished campaign.

## Mode separation checklist

| Mode | Learn from | Result should feel like | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premium | Klein and Galli | A polished, single-focus commercial artwork | Page layouts, notes, multiple scenes, typography |
| Editorial | Mac Conner | A period magazine story built around the preserved subject | An isolated hero poster or extra characters |
| Agency | George Lois archive | An internal idea-development presentation | A polished final advertisement with decorative fake process |

## Shared period qualities

Across all modes, look for cream paper, muted red, mustard, olive, navy, warm brown, gouache-like brushwork, ink variation, offset-print character, confident shapes, and deliberate negative space.

Avoid neon color, glossy 3D rendering, cinematic photorealism, modern social-media layouts, contemporary UI gradients, and generic sepia filtering.

# Subject-direction examples

These are transformation examples, not separate visual styles. Every category still uses the same Mad Men-era foundation and one of the three fixed modes.

## Person

For three detailed person cases, read [person.md](person.md).

Input: one person with a clear face and pose.

- **Premium:** a single-person commercial portrait with refined period lighting and no text.
- **Editorial:** the same person in a 1960s office, café, or domestic setting; the story comes from space and objects, never extra people.
- **Agency:** a monochrome hand-drawn campaign-direction board focused only on that person, with rough crop, wardrobe-material, and layout studies.

Pass only if identity, face, apparent age, pose, gesture, carried objects, and subject count remain unchanged.

## Pet

For three detailed pet cases, read [pet.md](pet.md).

Input: one pet with visible coat, markings, proportions, and pose.

- **Premium:** the pet as the sole hero of a refined companion-brand visual.
- **Editorial:** the same pet in a warm domestic setting with everyday objects and no owner or additional animal.
- **Agency:** a monochrome hand-drawn campaign board with rough composition studies centered on the same pet.

## Architecture

For three detailed architecture cases, read [architecture.md](architecture.md).

Input: a building with a clear silhouette and defining geometry.

- **Premium:** a landmark-centered commercial artwork using bold shape and negative space.
- **Editorial:** the same building placed within a richer city-life composition without adding unrelated people.
- **Agency:** a monochrome hand-drawn city or travel campaign board with rough poster-direction and crop studies.

The building must remain recognizable; a vintage filter alone is a failure.

## Food

Input: a clearly identifiable prepared food or packaged food item.

- **Premium:** one appetizing hero subject in a finished commercial painting with no text.
- **Editorial:** the same food in a period table or kitchen setting, using objects and light for narrative.
- **Agency:** a monochrome hand-drawn campaign board exploring serving context and composition without inventing ingredients or brands.

## Product

Input: a product with visible form, packaging, proportions, and identifying structure.

- **Premium:** a single-product hero composition with high finish and no typography.
- **Editorial:** the unchanged product integrated into a period lifestyle setting.
- **Agency:** a monochrome hand-drawn campaign-development board exploring presentation and use context, not redesigning the product or packaging.
